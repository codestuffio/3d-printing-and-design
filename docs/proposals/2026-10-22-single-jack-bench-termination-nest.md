# Single-jack bench termination nest

Date: 2026-10-22. Category: 10-inch rack homelab accessories. Proposal only; CAD requires separate user approval.

## Intended use and evidence

Support one measured punch-down keystone jack on a workbench while preparing and terminating cable for a small rack patch panel. The proposed benefit is fewer jack repositioning attempts during a repeated bench task. The jack sits in a shallow open nest, with its termination area exposed and its cable leaving through an open channel. Two broad outside ears let existing bench clamps secure the fixture without squeezing the jack.

[trueCABLE's termination stand](https://www.truecable.com/products/handheld-termination-aid) establishes commercial holder prior art and explicitly limits tested compatibility to its own jack family. [Its termination guide](https://www.truecable.com/blogs/cable-academy/how-to-terminate-an-unshielded-punch-down-keystone-jack) documents the conductor-dressing and punch-down task. [HCI's stand](https://www.hci.com.tw/en/product/Tool_3013AFT.html) uses molded high-impact ABS for specified jacks and a 110 tool. These are existing manufactured products, not evidence that a printed bench fixture withstands the same work. No novelty, universal fit, protection or network-performance claim is made; develop original geometry from the actual hardware.

No owner jack, tool, recurring termination task or handling difficulty has been confirmed. Practical-value confidence is low, about 25%; geometric complexity appears low, while impact behavior is unresolved. First compare the actual jack manufacturer's recommended holder and support method. A commercial stand may make this print unnecessary. Compare a simple carved or drilled wood support, or an existing holder fixed to wood, when compatible with the jack instructions. A nonfunctional mockup can test access before CAD. Stop if the real workflow is in the rack, cable slack cannot reach a bench, or setup takes more effort than the holder saves. This is separate from the existing rack cable saddle, adapter locator and plug-latch paddle, and from the discarded screw-starting fork.

## Smallest practical design

One original solid base with a shallow model-specific recess, broad housing-bearing lands, an open cable exit and two external clamp ears. Keep the jack's latch, mating opening, contact cavity, conductor slots, wiring label and full tool approach clear. The recess locates the housing loosely; it must not snap in, squeeze it or rely on a fragile latch for restraint. The proposed load path runs from a jack housing surface suitable for support, through the bearing lands and base, into the bench. If the jack instructions and measured geometry do not provide a suitable support surface, reject the design.

No interchangeable inserts, vise mechanism, rack mounting, printed blade, impact mechanism or electrical contact. Bench clamps act on the ears, away from the jack and cable. Clamps restrain the base; they do not establish that the loosely located jack stays seated under every prescribed punch direction. Confirm that the support faces and recess prevent jack rocking, rotation or ejection at each termination position. Reject the arrangement if this requires squeezing the jack or using its latch as a stop. The fixture must remain flat after clamping and allow the terminated jack to lift out without pulling individual wires. Use only disconnected spare hardware during development. Keep hands outside the punch tool's working path; the print is a positioning fixture, not protective equipment. Follow the actual jack and tool instructions throughout.

## Parametric OpenSCAD approach after approval

Extrude a rounded base and union broad ears, then subtract the measured housing recess, latch relief and edge-open cable channel. Use named top-level millimeter parameters for base length/width/thickness, jack housing envelope, recess depth/clearance, support-land positions/widths, latch relief, cable/boot exit envelope, tool-clearance envelope, ear size and edge radii. A generic rectangular housing is insufficient if the actual jack has steps or unsupported protrusions; parameterize only the selected measured model.

Guard positive dimensions, intact connected bearing lands and ear joins, minimum remaining floor, an uninterrupted cable/removal path and rounding that preserves the intended contacts. Exercise actual small/large clearance cases and intended rejections for erased supports, closed exits, negative dimensions and tool-envelope intersections. Export a same-source short contact coupon before the complete fixture. Digital checks must inspect support locations, recess depth and access envelopes as well as strict connected, closed, consistently wound, finite, nondegenerate positive-volume meshes and bounds. A coupon cannot establish full-base stability or impact behavior.

## Material, hardware and measurements

PLA is a candidate for a short fit coupon. PETG is a candidate for a bench prototype only after stiffness, clamping and controlled tool-load trials; neither has an established impact rating here. Candidate hardware is two existing suitable bench clamps. Their jaws and pads must bear fully on the ears with space for normal operation. Do not infer printed performance from molded ABS. Print orientation, walls, infill and supports would be chosen after examining the actual load path and slicer toolpaths, not from a generic profile.

Record the exact jack and punch tool models, manufacturer support/termination instructions and permitted reuse, housing steps and robust support surfaces, latch and contact keep-outs, conductor access, blade orientation and full tool sweep. Measure cable diameter, exit/bend requirements, available slack, jack lift-out path, bench/clamp geometry, printer/nozzle/layers/material and measurement resolution. Confirm an actual batch and useful improvement target. Fixture clearance must not force extra untwisting or alter the specified wire dressing or strain relief. No default rack, jack or punch-force dimensions are assumed.

## Acceptance and physical validation after approval

Before CAD approval, establish the real task and strongest existing-holder baseline, including retrieval, setup, dressing, termination, removal and cleanup. Check access with a mockup. Keep the existing support if it already works comfortably or if the measured housing cannot be supported without obstructing the tool or stressing a latch.

After CAD approval, validate meaningful parameter cases and actual support/access geometry, inspect slicing, and print the contact coupon. Fit a spare unconnected jack without force; reject housing marks, latch deformation, blocked slots or a tight removal path. Print one full fixture only after that passes. With spare hardware, check clamping, flatness, cable exit, tool approach and jack removal without loading the tool. Then conduct controlled trials at every required punch position and blade orientation, using the actual tool and its prescribed settings on a clear bench. Stop on any fixture motion, jack tilt, clamp interference, cracking, permanent set, housing/IDC damage or tool contact with the fixture. Do not proceed to service cabling until the bench arrangement passes; use remains on the bench.

Compare ten matched terminations per method with equivalent new spare jacks and cable sections, alternating method order; do not repeatedly punch the same IDC unless its manufacturer expressly permits that reuse. Agree on a worthwhile improvement before testing. Record complete task time, repositioning/retries, consumable waste and defects. Require the chosen improvement without worse total time, wire dressing, termination quality or handling. Use the cable/jack maker's appropriate inspection and test procedure; a basic wire-map pass alone does not establish category performance or certification.

Inspect after twenty additional jack placement/removal and clamp cycles, recording ear/floor wear, bowing and debris. These unloaded cycles do not establish impact life; record actual loaded terminations separately and remain within the demonstrated conditions.
