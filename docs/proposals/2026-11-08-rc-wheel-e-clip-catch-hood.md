# Bench catch hood for RC wheel E-clips

**November 8, 2026 · Proposal only**

## When it would help

On some RC airplanes, a small E-clip retains the wheel on its axle. A clip that slips free of the removal tool can bounce off the bench. A catch hood would keep one clip visible and retrievable during repeated service on a spare, detached wheel and axle assembly.

The E-flite Viper 64 mm EDF manual lists three wheel-retaining E-clips for 2.5 mm axles. This confirms the hardware exists on that model. The listed axle diameter alone gives neither the clip's outside dimensions nor the tool fit. [E-flite Viper 64 mm EDF manual, page 20](https://www.horizonhobby.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-horizon-master/default/dw9985737d/Manuals/EFL07750_EFL07775_MANUAL_EN.pdf#page=20)

First try the correctly sized metal tool over a tray with a clear bag around the work. A documented RC car example uses a bag to catch a wheel clip. Use the appropriate tool for the actual airplane hardware in both methods. Pursue the hood only if a recurring wheel-service task is confirmed and the bag repeatedly gets in the way or makes retrieval difficult. A tool that holds the clip securely may already solve the problem. [RC Car Action's clear-bag example](https://www.rccaraction.com/articles/tiny-e-clip-catch)

## Smallest useful design

Print a shallow basin with a light-colored floor and rails for a removable clear PET sheet. An axle relief and a short tool opening give a straight view and enough room for the existing tool to engage and withdraw. Support the detached assembly independently so the hood carries neither the wheel nor the removal force. The lid lifts or slides clear for retrieval after the tool is out. Keep the print on the bench. The hood collects loose hardware and provides no rated protection.

If approved, model the rounded basin and lid rails as millimeter-parametric OpenSCAD extrusions. Expose pocket size and depth, wall thickness, tool opening and axle relief, and sheet thickness and rail clearance. Print a small sample of the rail and tool opening before the basin. Try light-colored PETG and an off-the-shelf clear PET sheet with smooth edges. The first design needs no fasteners.

## Measurements and checks

- Identify the exact model, permitted service procedure and tool. Measure the detached assembly, actual clip width and thickness, axle projection, tool approach and withdrawal sweep, independent support height, and the openings around the lid, axle and tool. Leave enough radial room for the clip to clear the groove. Check each gap with the tool in place and withdrawn. A clip can turn edge-on. [Retaining-ring removal guidance](https://tg-eugene.com/blog/how-to-remove-retaining-ring/)
- Export one closed, connected basin. Check its dimensions and exercise valid and rejected parameter cases for broken walls, blocked tool access and loose or impossible lid fit. Inspect the slice for usable rails and clean openings. Check the printed sample and then dry-fit the full hood with a spare assembly.
- In a separate clear enclosure and with eye protection, alternate ten removals per method using the same tool and equivalent spare hardware. Keep clip condition comparable and replace parts as the service procedure requires. Record escapes from each catcher, failed tool engagement, accidental contact and time from setup through retrieval. Try the allowed approach extremes and inspect where clips rebound or hide. Advance only if the hood catches every clip in the trials, allows full tool travel while the hood stays clear of the assembly, and shows a repeatable access or retrieval gain over the bag. Inspect the lid and rails for loosening after twenty additional handling cycles.

A compatible owner assembly, recurring clip loss, and a gap with the clear-bag method are unconfirmed. CAD, slicing, fit, capture and wear await separate approval and testing.
