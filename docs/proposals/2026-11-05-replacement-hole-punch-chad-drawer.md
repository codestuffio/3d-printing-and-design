# Replacement hole-punch chad drawer — November 5, 2026

Proposal only. CAD approval pending. Category: home office.

## Use and evidence

Restore chip collection on one otherwise usable manual desktop paper punch whose separate waste drawer is missing or cracked. The printed drawer would catch the punched paper discs, pull out by an external grip and empty over a bin. This is a repair proposal for an existing failure. No owned punch, failed drawer, recurring paperwork task or unavailable spare has been identified.

The [Rapesco P1100/P2200 manual, printed pages 5–6](https://rapesco.com/kd733/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Manual-for-P1100P2200_2024.pdf#page=5) identifies the metal chassis separately from the chip container and describes withdrawing the drawer and opening its lid to empty it. That establishes a real drawer workflow, not dimensions or compatibility with this proposal. The [Rapesco 827](https://rapesco.com/product/827-2-hole-metal-punch-silver/) instead has a confetti tray and full rubber base. A rigid print must not replace a tray that also supplies footing, structural support or a guard. No novelty, universal fit or manufacturer endorsement is claimed.

First identify an actual failed drawer and ask the manufacturer about the correct spare or warranty repair. Compare the existing workaround, such as a shallow paper-lined container where the tool instructions permit it. Stop if no repeated task exists, an ordinary repair is practical, or the workaround catches chips cleanly without creating extra handling. Keep an intact drawer in use.

## Smallest practical design

One fixed-size shallow basin with rounded internal corners, measured guide surfaces, an insertion stop and an external pull lip. Retain the original lid if intact and compatible. If it is missing too, add one simple removable cover only after measuring the fixed waste outlets, original containment and insertion envelope. Preserve the necessary inlet openings and closure. An open tray is suitable only when the actual tool's enclosure provides the original containment during use and withdrawal. The manufacturer example's lid must not be assumed optional.

Use existing drawer receivers without modifying the punch. Original feet, chassis, cutters, punch boards, paper guides, sheet capacity and guarding remain the tool's responsibility. Exclude powered punches, sensor/interlock interfaces, flexible snap-on base trays and any layout where the container supports punching force or performs an unverified protective function. Stop if the required lid, outlet path, retention or moving-part clearance cannot be preserved with this small scope.

## Parametric approach after approval

Create original millimeter OpenSCAD geometry using extruded rounded profiles and a subtracted basin, with editable body width/depth/height, floor and wall thickness, corner radii, receiver profile/clearance, stop position and pull-lip reach. A required cover gets named outlet positions/sizes and closure clearances. Generate a short receiver-and-stop coupon from the same contact geometry before the full container. Guard positive dimensions, remaining walls, receiver engagement, inlet margins and insertion clearances. Export each part separately and keep STLs in ignored exports.

PLA is a candidate for a cool indoor fit coupon; PETG is a candidate for the finished basin and cover. Neither material has proven sliding wear, static behavior or chip release here. Expect no purchased hardware for a compatible straight-slide interface. If the actual tool needs a hinge, fastener or flexible latch, reassess scope before implementation. Slicer inspection must check flat orientation, layer direction at the pull lip, lid fit, bridges and sliding surfaces. No print settings or tolerances are established yet.

## Measurements and checks

Record the exact punch model/revision, failed part and any surviving original or manufacturer drawing. Measure the receiver cross-section and full travel, stop and retention features, collection cavity, fixed chip outlets, lid contact/closure, pull access, bench contact plane and the entire operating clearance envelope. Verify which parts carry force and provide guarding. Measure printer error and choose coupon clearance from the actual machine/material. A product photo supplies none of these fit dimensions.

After separate approval, require closed connected meshes for the basin and each cover/coupon, meaningful smaller/larger valid parameter cases, and invalid cases for negative dimensions, vanishing walls, blocked waste openings and an out-of-envelope receiver. Check intended dimensions and waste passage as well as mesh closure. Inspect slicing, print the coupon, then test full insertion and withdrawal with the handle stationary before a complete prototype.

Physical acceptance: the drawer seats and retains without force or play that spills chips, leaves the original base stable and clears all operating parts. Follow the actual tool instructions. Check collection using a small paper batch within its rating, then staged fills up to an agreed conservative emptying level. Track escaped and retained chips, inlet blockage, accidental drawer motion and spills during withdrawal, carrying, opening and tipping. Empty it promptly. Confirm the cover performs its required containment and that all chips can be removed without reaching into the mechanism.

Compare ten matched punch-and-empty batches per permitted method, alternating order and recording paper type/count, chips escaped, cleanup and total handling time. A printed replacement earns its place only if it restores reliable containment and easier handling than the actual workaround, after including measuring, fitting, fabrication and storage burden. A correct manufacturer spare is the preferred restoration baseline if available. Twenty extra insertion/emptying cycles can screen for wear, cracks, loosening and static buildup; these proposed counts establish neither lifetime nor statistical proof. No fair baseline means no superiority claim.
